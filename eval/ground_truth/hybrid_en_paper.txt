A Study of Gradient Dynamics in Deep
Residual Networks
Deep residual networks have become the dominant architecture for a broad range of perception tasks. In this work we revisit the optimization geometry of residual connections and derive improved scaling rules for very d
eep networks. We train networks of depth 50 to 200 layers on image classification benchmarks and observe consistent gains.
1. Introduction
Residual learning reframes the mapping problem: instead of fitting a desired underlying mapping H(x) directly, the stacked layers fit a residual F(x) = H(x) - x. This reformulation eases optimization and enables training 
of networks that are substantially deeper than previously possible. Prior work has studied normalization and initialization; here we focus on the interaction between residual branches and learning-rate schedules.
2. Related Work
Highway networks introduced gated shortcut connections, while identity shortcuts were popularized by residual networks. Later analyses connected residual flows to ordinary differential equations, motivating neural OD
E solvers. Our analysis complements this line by quantifying the variance of gradient updates across depth.
3. Method
y_l = x_l + F(x_l, W_l)
x_(l+1) = lambda * x_l + mu * F(x_l, W_l)
We introduce two scalar hyper-parameters lambda and mu that control the trade-off between the identity path and the residual branch. When lambda equals one and mu equals one we recover the standard residual block. 
Setting lambda greater than one effectively rescales the network depth, and we prove a variance preservation condition under which gradients neither vanish nor explode.

5. Conclusion
We presented a simple reparameterization of residual networks with provable variance preservation and consistent empirical gains. The approach requires no extra parameters and composes with existing normalization sc
hemes.
