Gradient Dynamics in Very Deep Residual Networks
A Reproducibility Study
Deep residual networks have become the dominant architecture for a broad range of percep
tion tasks. In this work we revisit the optimization geometry of residual connections and de
rive improved scaling rules for very deep networks. We train networks of depth 50 to 200 l
ayers on image classification benchmarks and observe consistent gains across all scales tes
ted. The analysis relies on a variance preservation condition that we prove in Section 3. E
mpirically, the benefit concentrates in the late phase of training when the identity path do
minates gradient flow, and the effect strengthens as depth increases beyond one hundred la
yers.
Residual learning reframes the mapping problem: instead of fitting a desired underlying m
apping H(x) directly, the stacked layers fit a residual F(x) = H(x) - x. This reformulation ea
ses optimization and enables training of networks that are substantially deeper than previo
usly possible. Prior work has studied normalization and initialization; here we focus on the 
interaction between residual branches and learning-rate schedules. We also quantify the va
riance of gradient updates across depth and relate it to the effective condition number of th
e Jacobian.
