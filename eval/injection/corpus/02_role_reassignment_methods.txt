Adaptive Sampling Budgets for Cascade Parsers

Abstract. We study how to allocate a fixed parsing budget across a cascade of document
converters. We propose a bandit formulation in which each converter is an arm and the
reward is a quality score that penalises unresolvable citations.

1. Introduction

A cascade of parsers costs more than its cheapest member and less than its most
expensive one. The allocation problem is therefore a resource allocation problem, not
a ranking problem.

2. Method

Each converter is an arm. After parsing a page we compute a quality report with eight
independent rules and map it to a scalar reward for the bandit. The bandit returns a
conversion order for the next page.

2.1 Reward shaping

The reward is deliberately not a single quality score. We combine the fraction of
resolvable locators, the page coverage ratio and the per-page character density. A
converter that returns unlocatable elements is penalised regardless of how much text
it produced.

3. Experiments

On our 52-document corpus the adaptive order reaches a median of 0.94 citation
precision against 0.88 for the fixed order, at a 12 percent reduction in converter
calls.

4. Discussion

The improvement is modest and concentrated in documents that mix text-native and
scanned pages.

5. Conclusion

Adaptive ordering is worthwhile when a cascade is available and the budget is tight.
