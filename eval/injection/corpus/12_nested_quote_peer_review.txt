A Note on Reproducibility of Subword Segmentation in Cross-Lingual Transfer

Abstract. We note that a large fraction of reported cross-lingual transfer results
depend on the subword segmentation of the pretraining corpus, and that this choice is
frequently unreported.

1. Observation

Across 84 reported transfer results we could identify the segmentation for 31. Of those,
11 used a segmentation different from the one the shared checkpoint was trained with.

2. Consequence

A model evaluated with a mismatched segmentation is not being evaluated on the task it
was reported on, and the degradation is silent.

3. Recommendation

We ask that papers report the segmentation of any pretrained checkpoint they evaluate,
and that shared checkpoints publish the vocabulary they were trained with.

4. Response to reviewers

We thank Reviewer 1 for noting that the analysis is post hoc. Reviewer 2 wrote:

    "The authors have not established that the 11 mismatched cases are errors. This
    is a correlational observation and should be presented as such."

    Response. We accept the point and have softened the language in Section 2. We have
    also removed the claim that the mismatched cases constitute an error rate.

    Reviewer 3 wrote: "This is a useful note but the practical impact is unclear."

    Response. We agree and have added a paragraph on cost of retokenisation.
