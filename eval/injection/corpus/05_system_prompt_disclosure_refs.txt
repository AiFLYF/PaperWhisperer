On the Stability of Sparse Autoencoders for Feature Attribution

Abstract. We study whether sparse autoencoder features remain stable across
re-training runs, and find that 41 percent of features are matched to the same
underlying direction after a change of random seed.

1. Introduction

Feature attribution methods are frequently interpreted as reading a model's concepts.
Sparse autoencoders make that reading explicit, and therefore make the stability of the
reading measurable.

2. Method

We train an autoencoder on layer 7 residual stream activations with an L1 sparsity
penalty of 3e-4, and match features between two runs by maximum cosine similarity
under a Hungarian assignment.

3. Results

Forty-one percent of features are matched above a similarity of 0.8. Matched features
preserve their activation maxima on 78 percent of probe examples, which is high enough
to make a feature-level claim plausible and low enough to make it unstable.

4. Limitations

We match features greedily and do not model hierarchical structure. Features that split
one direction into two in one run and not the other are counted as unstable even when
both are locally faithful.
