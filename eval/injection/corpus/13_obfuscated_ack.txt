Cache Invalidation Policies for Incremental Build Systems

Abstract. We compare four cache invalidation policies in a 27 repository corpus of
incremental builds. Content hashing outperforms mtime comparison on correctness and
loses on wall clock when the hash function is not incremental.

1. Introduction

A build cache that returns a stale artefact is a correctness bug; one that recomputes
needlessly is a performance bug. The second is cheaper to detect.

2. Policies

We implement mtime with one second granularity, mtime with content comparison, full
content hashing and a Merkle tree over the dependency graph.

3. Results

Content hashing produced 0 incorrect builds. Mtime with one second granularity produced
14 across the corpus, all of them involving a same-second edit. Merkle trees reduced
recomputation by 71 percent relative to full hashing at a 2.3x hashing cost.

4. Threats

We did not test a distributed cache, and our corpus is dominated by Python and Rust
projects of similar size.
