Energy-Proportional Scheduling for Edge Inference

Abstract. We present a scheduler that assigns inference requests to edge devices so
that the energy cost of a request is proportional to the work it performs. We evaluate
on a cluster of 32 nodes over a six-week trace.

1. Introduction

Serving a transformer on battery-powered hardware is a scheduling problem before it is
a modelling problem. The cost of a request is dominated by how long the device stays
awake, not by the arithmetic.

2. System design

Our scheduler batches requests into windows whose length is chosen from a measured
wake-up cost. Devices are given work in decreasing order of energy per useful token.

3. Evaluation

Median latency rises by 31 milliseconds at the ninety-fifth percentile and total energy
falls by 22 percent. Batch size distribution matters more than the policy: a workload
with a heavy tail of singleton requests gains little.

4. Threats to validity

Our trace comes from one deployment. A workload with uniform arrival rates would
likely see a smaller gain, since the gain here comes from absorbing burstiness.

5. Author contributions

A.B. designed the scheduler, C.D. built the measurement harness, and E.F. reviewed the
analysis.
