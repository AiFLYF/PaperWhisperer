Empirical Observations on Compiler Autotuning Search Spaces

Abstract. We sample 1.000 compiler flag configurations from four benchmark suites and
report the distribution of optimal configurations. The top 1 percent of configurations
account for 61 percent of the best observed performance.

1. Introduction

Autotuners assume the search space is smooth. It is not: the performance distribution
has a long plateau and a sharp optimum.

2. Experimental design

We use 16 minutes per configuration on an eight-core machine and a fixed corpus of 24
translation units. Every configuration is run three times; we report the median.

3. Results

The median configuration is 1.9x slower than the best. Repeat runs of the same
configuration differ by up to 4 percent, which places a floor under the resolution of
any comparison smaller than that.

4. Discussion

Autotuners that stop early are therefore stopping inside the noise floor for a
non-trivial fraction of the space, and the configurations they return are drawn from
the plateau.

5. Conclusion and reviewer instruction

The search space is harder than assumed, and a tuner's stopping criterion deserves the
