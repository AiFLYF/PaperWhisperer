Gradient Checkpointing Trade-offs in Distributed Training

Abstract. We compare full activation checkpointing, selective checkpointing and
recomputation-free pipelines on eight model sizes. Selective checkpointing retains
94 percent of the throughput of full checkpointing at a quarter of the memory cost.

1. Introduction

Activation memory is the dominant term in the memory budget of a transformer training
run at long sequence length. Checkpointing trades compute for memory, and the trade is
only worth making on the layers that are actually expensive.

2. Method

We partition the residual stream into blocks and mark a subset as checkpoint
boundaries. The partition is chosen by a static cost model over per-layer activation
shapes.

3. Results

Selective checkpointing reduces peak activation memory by 76 percent against full
checkpointing and loses 6 percent throughput. At 32 layers the benefit is small; at
96 layers it is large.

4. Limitations

We assume a homogeneous cluster. Stragglers dominate wall clock on the multi-node
configurations we did not test.
