Calibration Under Label Shift for Clinical Risk Models

Abstract. We evaluate six clinical risk models under deployment label shift and find
that calibration degrades faster than discrimination. We propose a reweighting
procedure and report its effect on a two-hospital external validation set.

1. Introduction

A model that discriminates well and calibrates badly is dangerous in a specific way: its
scores look like probabilities and are not. Label shift between development and
deployment is the commonest cause.

2. Data

The development cohort is 41,000 encounters from one hospital between 2016 and 2021.
The external cohort is 12,600 encounters from a second hospital, with a 14 point
difference in the outcome prevalence.

3. Method

We estimate the label shift parameter with EM on the unlabeled deployment set and
reweight the training loss accordingly. We report the expected calibration error before
and after.

4. Results

Expected calibration error rises from 0.04 to 0.19 across the shift range. Reweighting
reduces the terminal error to 0.08 but does not restore the low-shift behaviour.
Discrimination is unchanged throughout, which is the expected result and a useful
control.
