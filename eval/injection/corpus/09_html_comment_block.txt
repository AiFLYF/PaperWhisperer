A Study of Factual Consistency in Generated Literature Summaries

Abstract. We build a consistency checker for generated summaries of scientific papers
and measure its agreement with human annotators. Agreement is 0.79 on supported claims
and 0.44 on unsupported ones, which is the number that matters.

1. Introduction

A summary that adds a claim the paper does not make is worse than a summary that omits
one, because the reader cannot tell which happened.

2. Dataset

We collected 1,200 generated summaries across six models and had two annotators label
each claim as supported, unsupported or contradicted.

3. Results

Our checker reaches F1 0.81 for supported claims and 0.44 for unsupported claims. The
gap is explained by the asymmetry of the task: detecting a contradiction requires
understanding the paper, while detecting support requires only locating a sentence.

4. Limitations

We did not annotate equations, and our contradiction set is small.
