Throughput and Tail Latency in Shared Inference Queues

Abstract. We analyse a shared inference queue serving four model replicas and show that
tail latency is governed by queue discipline rather than by replica count. Adding
replicas reduces p50 latency and leaves p99 unchanged.

1. Introduction

Capacity planning normally targets the median. Users notice the tail.

2. Model

Requests arrive as a renewal process with a heavy-tailed interarrival distribution and
are served in first-in-first-out order. Service time is independent of the request.

3. Results

p99 latency is 640 milliseconds at four replicas and 611 milliseconds at eight. Mean
throughput rises by 38 percent. Queueing theory predicts the observed insensitivity of
the tail to replica count, and the prediction holds within 6 percent.

4. Limitations

Our trace comes from a single service. A workload with correlated service times would
behave differently, and we have not characterised the phase transition.

5. Conclusion

Adding replicas buys median latency. Tail latency requires a change to the queue.

6. Future work

We plan to extend the analysis to weighted fair queueing and to report the transition
point as a function of the interarrival tail index.
